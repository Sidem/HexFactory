    Hub requests · insight

    The landing hub pays insight for what it asks for here, and for nothing else. Deliver with X beside the hub, or point a belt at it.

      Moving and looking

      Move with WASD, and hold Shift to walk slowly when you want to stop on one particular hex. The player always faces the pointer. Space centres the camera back on the player, T pauses and resumes the factory, M mutes the feedback sounds, and dragging with the middle button — or with Shift held and the left one — pans while the wheel zooms. The right button is never the camera: it is how you work a hex.

      Panels

      B opens the construction catalogue, I the cargo pack, O research, and P this panel. Esc closes whatever is open and returns to the inspect tool, which is the one panel that stays on the world.

      Field actions

      Hold F to keep gathering from the field you are standing on or beside, and X to hand the landing hub whatever it is asking for. To work one particular hex, hold the right button on it: holding keeps harvesting the cell you are pointing at, as far as your own reach goes, and dragging walks the harvest along to the next one. The ring that closes around you is the wait until the next action.

      The bar, and what goes on it

      B opens the catalogue: everything you can build, grouped by what it is for, each card showing what it costs and — for a machine — every recipe it runs, written as the materials in and the materials out. Click a recipe to hold that machine set to it. The bottom bar is yours to arrange: 1–9 select its nine slots, Pin drops a building into the first free one, and dragging a card onto a slot chooses which. Drag a slot onto another to swap them, or use the × to clear one. Your arrangement is remembered.

      Building

      With a belt or single-hex building selected, drag across the map to lay a whole run at once — it routes itself, turning where the drag turns. E selects erase, and dragging with it removes a run. R rotates the pending building, or the one under the cursor when no build tool is held. Q copies whatever is under the cursor, and Ctrl+Z takes back the last thing you built. Machines run one category of recipe each; smelters and kilns also need fuel, and burn whatever arrives. Risers are belts that run due north and due south, over the seam between two hexes — the hexes they cross stay free to build on.

      Boxes and tiers

      Click a container to see everything in it. Each stored row has a Take, and each stack you are carrying has a Put, so stock moves both ways without a belt. Buildings with a higher tier show an Upgrade button that grows them in place, keeping their contents, their heading, and everything connected to them.

      Reading the ground

      Terrain is the material map, and hatched ground is ground you cannot stand on. Travelling toward the dashed survey frontier lifts the fog for good; the minimap and the gold marker on the edge of the view both point back to the landing hub.

      World parameters

      Feature scale is how big a landform is; a band level is how much of the world it covers. Raising the sea level makes more water, not bigger water — bigger water is a larger landform scale. The resource table comes from the preset.

      Named saves remember the world you started and the build that wrote them. An older run stays in the list; if this version cannot load it, the row says why.

# Mechanical components — v0.33.0

Recorded 2026-08-28. This record owns the component/contract slice of phase 1: recipe and reward
arithmetic, the compatibility boundary, and its verification. The remaining economy audit is not
complete; ground works, oil and floors remain later work.

## Recipe and commission

One iron plate plus one gear makes one component, a plate-mounted mechanical mechanism. The
recipe keeps ID 1, its one-component output and eight industrial ticks (32 attended workshop
ticks). A gear still costs two plates. Prove the line now asks for one component instead of three,
so the commission still needs six ore, not eighteen. Neither primitive station needs research,
power, copper or crystal. The foundry-stage bill and all building prices are unchanged.

The furnace costs six stone and four clay; the workshop costs four wood and two stone. Smelt
three plates, cut two into a gear, then mount that gear on the remaining plate. Smelting burns
240 energy: two coal leave 80 banked energy, or six wood supply exactly the heat. Delivery grants
the same four starter automation technologies, without charging or refunding insight.

The before column is the v0.32 committed balance fixture, checked with `npm run balance` before
editing. The after column is regenerated by the native balance fixture and independently checked
by TypeScript arithmetic.

The wood-fuel route needs 28 gathers including station materials. This is a deliberately deeper
opening, not a claim that it takes the same time or feels better. Gathering, machine and attended
work are separate costs; their clocks are not an elapsed playtime measurement. Walking, stock
handling and operator delay remain excluded. Full commission/research startup accounting for
later stations remains outstanding, including finite request payout replay and grid operating
fuel. No human timing validation is owed; that requirement was withdrawn.

The component request still takes eight, preserving partially delivered rows. Its reward rises
26 → 98 insight: expanded demand rises from 16 ore to 48 ore plus 12 coal, or 16 → 60 gathers.
That preserves the previous reward per gather (1.625 → 1.633 after integer rounding). Recipe work
rises 64 → 368 industrial ticks, so the report's theoretical insight/minute falls 51.316 → 46.372.
Repeat payouts, all other requests and research prices are unchanged. This is not the planned
finite-project income redesign. Existing components and partially filled request rows receive
the current reward; this is a bounded stock revaluation, not a reverse-conversion recipe.

## Compatibility and recovery

Save 25 / definitions 20 / scenarios 7; technologies 11, world 8 and wire 13 are unchanged.
The adjacent envelope migration leaves original state and checksum untouched. After checksum
verification, ordinary contract advancement consumes one already-contributed component and grants
the starter technologies if needed. A stage-zero save with one or two contributions advances;
unused contribution stays credited. A fresh, completed or victorious factory is not reset, and
reloading the migrated save cannot consume or grant twice. Insight, request history and delivered
counters are preserved.

An active legacy recipe has already reserved its two ore. Its unchanged duration/output let it
finish once; canceling by demolition returns the actual reserved ore, not the new plate-and-gear
bill. Unreserved ore stays in the input compartment and can be withdrawn. Further jobs require
the new ingredients. Legacy automated component lines therefore need upstream plate and gear
supplies; the loader does not conjure those supplies or rewrite the player's factory. The saved
request quantity stays eight, so an old partial delivery remains valid.

New Factory demo runs use wood → cutter → timber on the same turning transport path, powered by
the existing wind turbine. Existing demo saves retain their original blueprint and stock.
The capacity harness separately pins its historical ore recipe in its bench-only catalogue;
its unchanged checksum and delivery assertion prove that this release did not silently turn
performance tests into idle-machine tests. No new performance claim is made.

Guidance starts with the primitive furnace, then the workshop, and describes intermediate work.
Carried contract goods take priority over rebuilding their production stations, including legacy
components whose supplier has been dismantled. The save picker recognizes both adjacent scenario
migrations and continues to reject unknown versions.

## Verification

Native regression tests cover the full commission with both fuel routes, current-job save/resume,
station rebuilding, partial/completed/victorious legacy commissions, checksum tampering, duplicate
grant prevention, and legacy manual/industrial job completion, withdrawal and cancellation.
The metered demo test checks real timber delivery and conservation, including in-flight stocks.
The balance fixture and independent TypeScript tests pin the opening and request arithmetic.

The complete local quality gate passed: zero dependency vulnerabilities, generated-map check,
formatting, lint, types, 253 TypeScript tests, 189 Rust tests, and production Wasm/Vite build.
The existing Vite large-chunk warning remains.

Browser QA loaded the existing save-24 Industrial Bills factory without overwriting its slot,
showed the one-component mission and furnace-first guidance, and kept older save Load controls
enabled. A fresh demo showed a powered cutter consuming wood and a receiving endpoint. In that
isolated demo a workshop was placed using creative mode, then ten existing plates were loaded:
cutting one gear left eight plates, and mounting it with creative mode switched off left seven
plates and one component. Both crafts completed once and returned to Awaiting player work.
The ingredient slots showed plate plus gear, the output slot showed Component, and the browser
reported no warnings or errors. Browser QA used pre-existing plates; the complete ore/fuel route
and partial-contribution migrations are covered by native tests, not claimed as browser playthroughs.
